# Tiling slices

Date: 2026-10-16 10:29 EDT

## Context

Super+T keeps switching a desktop between tiled and floating. On a tiled desktop every window holds a slice of
the desktop's area — the whole of it, a half or a quarter — and Super+arrows move and resize the focused window's
slice the way Windows' own snap keys do: Super+Left takes the left half; Super+Left then Super+Down, or Super+Down
then Super+Left, the bottom-left quarter; the opposite key widens a slice back first, so a window in the left half
grows to the whole width on Super+Right, and a window in the bottom-left quarter grows to the left half on
Super+Up. A slice that grows over other windows pushes them into the free space left. A new window takes the
largest free slice; with no free space it halves the largest slice. Free space stays free when a window closes,
until a window takes it. On a floating desktop Super+Up keeps toggling maximize. The rules go into
[canvas/desktops.md](../intents/canvas/desktops.md) and
[input/keyboard-and-pointer.md](../intents/input/keyboard-and-pointer.md).

The rules in full:

- The board is the desktop's area inside the bars, with the tiling gaps. Per axis a slice spans the first half,
  the whole, or the second half; a slice that only halving made (more windows than quarters) counts as the half
  it lies in.
- Super+Left: the second half becomes the whole width, the whole width the left half, the left half stays.
  Super+Right, Super+Up and Super+Down alike on their side and axis; the other axis keeps its span.
- Pushed windows, in reading order, each take the largest free slice; with none free, they halve the largest
  slice other than the one the key moved.
- A new window, and every window when tiling is switched on (in reading order of where they stood), takes the
  largest free slice; with none free, the largest slice is halved along its longer side, its holder keeping the
  left or top half.
- Closing, minimizing or moving a window out frees its slice; a restored window takes its slice back when it is
  free, else it is placed like a new one. Maximize and fullscreen keep the window's slice while they last.
- A tiled window dragged inside the desktop swaps slices with the window under the drop, or takes the largest
  free slice under it; dropped outside, it leaves the board.
- A change of the desktop's scale never re-lays out the board, as today.
