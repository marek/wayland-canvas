# The normal view pans only while Super is held

Date: 2026-10-18 14:36 EDT

## Context

In the normal view a drag or a 2-finger scroll over the empty canvas pans
the view with no modifier, so a stray drag on a desktop's background
slides the desktop off the screen and the user's place is lost; getting
back means finding the desktop again. Moving the view elsewhere, and
moving desktops, is the overview's job: there desktops are dragged by
their frame and a click on a free spot goes there.

The result of this work:

- In the normal view the pointer and the 2-finger scroll pan only while
  Super is held, as Super decides a 2-finger pinch. Without Super a drag
  or a scroll over the empty canvas does nothing, and a click there stays
  a click.
- A pan in the normal view is a look away, not a move: the output
  remembers the view it rested on before the pan — a desktop at its
  scale, or a free view — and a double tap of Super eases back to it.
- The overview is unchanged.

Settled with the user (1A, 2A, 3A, and no chord):

- The view stays where the pan leaves it when Super is let go, so what
  the pan revealed can be used; double Super goes back.
- Going back is an action of its own, `pan-back`, bound by default to
  the double Super tap only and changeable like every action
  (`canvas.conf`, the settings window).
- Super with a 2-finger scroll pans over windows too, as a Super pinch
  never reaches a client; a desktop full of windows has no empty canvas
  to scroll over. Super with a left drag still moves a window it starts
  on and pans only from the empty canvas.
- Super+Alt+arrows is the same look away: double Super goes back from
  it too.
- Any other move — a hop, going to a desktop, the overview, a Super
  pinch that pins the view — makes the new view the one double Super
  goes back to. With nothing panned, double Super does nothing.
- One tap is told from two by a compile-time interval. A single Super
  tap given an action waits out that interval before it runs; while the
  single tap runs nothing, the default, nothing waits.

This can be felt only on a TTY: nested under this machine's Hyprland,
Super with a left drag, a right drag, the wheel and Alt with the arrows
are the host's own bindings, and a lone Super tap passes through to the
host. The headless scenarios drive it through fake devices.
